Credits
The Templars have just retreated from Rhodes, their stronghold in the Mediterranean, and are seeking help 
from the courts of Europe.
Meanwhile, Malta lies sleepily in the middle of the sea, inhabited by a few people who live mostly by fishing. 
But at the heart of Malta is a secret that some of the island's inhabitants zealously guard. A building lives in 
symbiosis with the tides, appearing and disappearing with the rhythm of the moons. Because of this peculiarity, 
the Black Rose covets it, but for now, other forces are watching over it.
Alice has arrived from Milan with her dark passenger, sent by the Black Rose to steal the secrets of the building. 
But waiting for her is Alhazred, an ancient guardian, writer, and explorer, made immortal by his madness 
and symbiosis with the place.
Who will you side with? The Old Ones or the Black Rose?

HOW TO READ THIS RULEBOOK
This rulebook is divided into six chapters, which are to be consulted as indicated below in order to understand the game as simply 
and straightforwardly as possible.
1. Components: this chapter visually presents all the game components in the box.
2. Basics: this chapter introduces the game without going into the rules, but rather provides a foundation for better 
understanding the following chapters.
3. Setup: this chapter contains all the instructions for setting up the game area.
4. Game Turn: this chapter explains in detail the rules that come into play during the five phases that make up a game turn, 
clarifying the flow of actions that each player must put into practice, but without going into detail about rules that are not 
strictly related to a single game Phase, all of which are recalled by cross-references and directions to the chapter that contains 
them.
5. End of Game Tally: this chapter covers the rules for determining the winner at the end of the game.
6. Rules Compendium: this chapter explains in detail all the in-depth rules that affect the game continuously or that require 
a temporary interruption of the game flow in order to be applied.
7. Beyond the Duel: this chapter provides practical instructions for using this product in combination with the other 
products in the Black Rose Wars line.

Alhazred
Alice
Alhazred is a scholar. He is ageless.
He is said to have written not long ago the book about the undead, 
those who walk without breathing, the Kitab al-Azif, better known 
as the Necronomicon.
The Mad Arab has supported many caliphates with his powers 
over time and has been watching over Malta for several decades. 
During this time on the island he has identified a very powerful 
magical conjunction, an intersection of magical streams, of voices, 
that only the Perceivers can grasp.
Following these tides, he came to a semi-submerged cave where over 
the years he built his abode.
Living so long in this place he too has changed, and his appearance 
now resembles more that of a Mythos than that of a normal 
human being.
Don't let Alice's appearance fool you. 
She is not a child.
She has lived for over 100 years, deepening her studies of magic and 
the occult world. 
When she was a child trying to separate reality from the world 
beyond the veil, she merged her mind with that of a strange being.
Since then, her appearance hasn't changed and she lives in symbiosis 
with her dark passenger.
The presence of Xibille, that is her name, has implemented 
Alice's powers and now supports and protects her as their lives are 
inextricably linked.

Basics
Black Rose Wars Duel: Void is a game of mage duels set in the Black 
Rose Lodge, a sentient place where a powerful supernatural being, the 
Black Rose, resides. 
In this place, the most powerful mages in the world gather, learn, and 
challenge each other to determine who is the best among them, the one 
who will become the next Grand Magister of the Lodge.
This game allows two players to compete in a terrifying battle of magic 
and teaches the fundamentals of this game's older brother, Black Rose 
Wars. Everything in this box can be used with Black Rose Wars to 
expand the core game. 
This is a game that is independent of Black Rose Wars; most of the 
material in this box can be used to expand Black Rose Wars, however, 
the reverse is not always possible (see page 21).
MAGE CARD ANATOMY
 - The Mage
The Lodge and the Rooms
The Mage represents the player during the game. Each Mage has 
their own Model representing them in the Lodge and a Mage Card 
describing their characteristics.
Please note: On all game components, players are identified with 
the term "Mage."
The Lodge is the battleground where the mages duel during the 
game. The Lodge consists of 7 Rooms, which are placed on the 
board during game setup.
A Room has two states, Destroyed or Rebuilt. The side with the 
slots (called Instability Slots, see page 18) represents the Destroyed 
side and the reverse side represents Rebuilt Rooms.
The specific rules for Rooms and their Effects are explained in detail 
on page 19.

Setup
Follow the steps below to set up the game:
1 Open the game board A in the center of the Game Area.
2 Place the Deep Black Rose Room B in the center of the 
game board so that the Destroyed side is visible, that is, the side 
showing the Instability Slots (the holes).
Shuffle the remaining six Rooms and place them randomly 
around the Deep Black Rose Room so that the Destroyed side 
is visible; the set of seven Rooms is called the Lodge.
3 Place the Events Card C in the corresponding slot on the 
game board, so that one of the two sides chosen randomly or 
by mutual agreement is visible. Place the two Quest Cards D
in the corresponding slots of the game board, so that one of the 
two randomly or mutually chosen faces of each is visible.
4 Place the Room Activation Tokens E and the Black Rose's 
Damage/Instability Cubes (black cubes) F next to the 
Game Board, so that they are within reach of both players.
5 The player who last received a rose (or a randomly chosen 
player) gains the Crown Token () G , becoming the 
Crown Holder.
6 Starting with the Crown Holder and proceeding clockwise, 
each player chooses a color and sits on the side of the game board 
showing that color. They then take the Damage/Instability 
Cubes, Physical Action Tokens, Persistence Tokens, Power 
Token, and Evocation Bases corresponding to that color H . 
The player then chooses a Mage, takes the corresponding Model
and places it in the Cell I of their color. They take their Mage 
Card and place it on the corresponding slot on their side of the 
game board and place their Physical Action Tokens on it J . 
then they take their Mage's three Personal Spell Cards K and 
keep them in front of them, revealed.
7 Take the Void Spell School deck and place it next to the game 
board so that it is within reach of all players. Place the School 
Reference Card L under the School of Magic deck as shown 
in the image. This deck is considered the Library M .
Shuffle the available Forgotten Spells and create a deck; place 
it next to the game board in the corresponding slot N .
Place the Models of the Evocations, 3 Dagon, within reach O .
8 The Crown Holder takes the School Reference Card L
from the Library and selects one of the two Starting Grimoires 
E indicated on it. The player searches the School of Magic 
deck for the six cards indicated, then adds to them a Personal 
Spell Card of their own Mage and shuffles them. This seven￾card deck is their Grimoire; they place it in the corresponding 
slot on their side of the game board () P , then they take 
their other two Personal Spell Cards and place them revealed 
in the Memories slot () of their side of the game board Q . 
The other player does the same, however, choosing the Starting 
Grimoire that the Crown Holder did not choose.
Once this process is completed, shuffle the rest of the School of 
Magic deck of the Library and place it back on the side of the 
game board above the School Reference Card. 
9 Each player places one of their Damage/Instability Cube on slot 
0 of the Trophy Track and Room Track of their side of the 
game board R ; then places their own Power Token on slot 0 of 
the Power Track S .
The Crown Holder places a Damage/Instability Cube of the 
Black Rose on slot 0 of the Trophy Track, Room Track, and 
Turn Track of the game board T ; then they place the Black 
Rose Power Token on slot 0 of the Power Track U .
Now you are ready to play.

EFFECTS AND TARGETS
TARGETS
The Effects of Spells and other components 
of the game always have a specific target. That 
target must be chosen from those available by 
the player resolving the Effect. 
The target of an Effect is always indicated 
before the text of the Effect itself followed by 
a colon (:), except for the Spell Card Effect 
targets, which are listed in the third banner 
under the Spell name.
Effects resolved by a Mage with targets other 
than , unless specified in their text, never 
harm the Mage themselves and/or their 
Evocations. 
Targets may be:
•  Model: The Effect can target a Model other than itself. 
The number next to the icon (, , ) indicates the 
range, i.e., the number of Rooms of distance from the 
Model resolving the Effect in which the target might be; to 
choose a target, it must be possible to draw a straight line 
not blocked by the walls of the Rooms between the Room 
in which the target is and the Room in which the Model 
resolving the Effect is. If the icon is followed by an asterisk 
(), the Effect can target a Model in the Lodge regardless 
of distance, and even if an unbroken straight line cannot be 
drawn as indicated above.
•  Mage Model: As Model , but only a Mage's Model can 
be targeted.
•  Evocation Model: As Model , but only an Evocation's 
Model can be targeted.
•  Room: The Effect targets a Room and affects all 
Opposing Models (Mages and their Evocations) within that 
Room. The number within the icon (, , ) indicates 
the range, i.e., the number of Rooms away from the 
Model applying the Effect to the target Room; to choose 
the target Room, it must be possible to draw a straight 
line not blocked by the walls of the Rooms between it and 
the Room in which the Model resolving the Effect is. If 
the icon contains an asterisk (), the Effect can target a 
Lodge Room regardless of distance and even if an unbroken 
straight line cannot be drawn as indicated above.
•  Personal: This Effect targets the Mage who resolves it.
•  Special: No specific target. The text of the Effect 
indicates how to apply it and which elements of the game 
are affected by it.
For examples and in-depth rules on Targets, Range and 
Line of Sight see page 20.

RESOLUTION OF MULTIPLE EFFECTS
During the game, it may happen that more than one Effect 
needs to be resolved when a certain condition occurs, or while 
another effect is being resolved. When this happens, the Effect 
being resolved is resolved until the period (.), then the game 
stops and each Mage (or the ) in play order can resolve 1 or 
more Effects. When all Effects are resolved, the interrupted 
Effect resumes resolving from the period (.) at which it was 
interrupted.
EFFECTS
The game often refers to Effects. Most components of the game 
and every Physical Action (see page 13) have Effects, which are 
composed of one or more phrases and/or symbols that allow 
you to influence the game. Each phrase that makes up an Effect 
must be applied, starting with the first and proceeding in order. 
If a phrase cannot be applied in its entirety, the parts that cannot 
be applied (even all) are skipped and the game continues by 
applying the next phrase. An Effect is considered resolved
when an attempt has been made to apply all the phrases that 
make up the Effect, even if none of them could be applied.

3. Action Phase
The First Mage (the Crown Holder) performs up to two Actions 
of their choice from those shown in the Actions box on the right. 
When the First Mage has resolved all of their Actions, the other 
Mage does the same. This continues until both Mages no longer 
have the ability to perform Actions, then the Action Phase ends.
Note: if a Mage is in their Cell, they have a limited number of 
options available to them, see Cells on page 19.
During their Activation, a Mage must perform one or two of 
the Actions described in this box.

(see box on the left) one of their Available Physical Action

different group of Effects, and they are resolved in the 
order the Mage prefers; a Mage may also choose to ignore 
some or all of these Effects.

LIMITATION ON SPELL CASTING
A Mage may perform the Action "Cast a Spell" twice 
during the same Activation (using both of their Actions) 
only if one of the two Actions is used to cast the Spell that 
is in the  Quick Spell Slot.
ENTERING A ROOM
Regardless of how a Model is placed in a Room (Movement, 
Evocation, direct placement, or other), it is considered to 
have entered the Room. 
MOVE A MODEL IN THE LODGE
When a Model performs "Move," its controller moves it to an 
adjacent Room; they repeat this operation a number of times 
equal to the number indicated after the word "Move."
PLACE A MODEL IN THE LODGE
When a Model places itself (or is placed) in a Lodge Room, 
if it is not already in the Lodge, it is taken from the reserve. If 
that Model is already in the Lodge its position changes from 
the Room it was in directly to the Room in which it is placed, 
without passing through other Rooms.

SUMMONING EVOCATIONS
A Mage may summon an Evocation through different Effects.
A Mage who resolves an Effect that summons an Evocation 
takes the Model of that Evocation and places it in the Lodge, 
following the Effect's instructions. They then take the card of 
that Evocation and place it at one of the three Evocation slots on 
their side of the game board. They take the Evocation Base with 
the number of  indicated on the chosen Evocation Slot and 
clip it to the base of the Model; this will help associate a Model 
with the corresponding Evocation Card. In addition, if a Mage 
has two or more identical Models among their Evocations, the 
number of  helps to distinguish them when they are targeted 
by Effects.
If there are no available Models of a specific Evocation (because 
they are all in the Lodge), the Mage can still resolve the Effect 
that summons it, but does not place any Evocation in the Lodge.

EVOCATION
When a Mage summons an Evocation they are considered the 
Owner of that Evocation as long as that Model is clipped to an 
Evocation Base of the same color as the Mage. The ownership of 
a Model never changes.
The Controller of an Evocation is the Mage who makes any 
decisions related to that Evocation. By default, the owner of an 
Evocation is its controller.
During the course of the game, the controller of an Evocation 
may change due to various Effects. In this case, its ownership 
does not change and the new controller takes all decisions related 
to the Evocation. An Evocation that inflicts Damage or places 
Instability always places cubes of its controller's color.

RANGE
The numerical value of the targets (see page 11) is called the range, 
and it indicates the distance at which the target chosen to apply the 
Effect can be located. Ranges with a value greater than 0 ( ) 
require line-of-sight between the person resolving the Effect and 
the target.
A  range ( ) or one with a value of 0 ( ) instead, does 
not require any line of sight.
LINE OF SIGHT
The line of sight is an imaginary, straight line between the center of 
the Room in which the Model resolving the Effect is located and the 
center of the Room in which the target of the Effect or the Room 
targeted by the Effect is located. This line must always run through 
the centers of a row of Rooms, and can never run along the edge of 
a Room traversing a maximum number of Rooms (excluding the 
starting Room) equal to the range indicated on the Effect's target.
DUMMY TARGET

targets Models (). The Dummy Target counts as a Model, 
even if it is not physically in the Lodge; the purpose of choosing 
it as a target is to allow Mages to cast a Spell and resolve as much 
of its Effect as possible, even when there are no Models available 
as targets. A Dummy Target does not suffer Damage. It is not 
possible to choose a Dummy Model as the target of a Physical 
Attack. ().

D U E L I N B L AC K RO S E WA R S
The Material in this box can be used to expand Black Rose Wars by 
including it in games as shown below.
Void School of Magic: during step 9 of the Setup you may replace 
one of the Schools of Magic present in the core game with the Void 
School.
Forgotten Spells: you may add these cards to the Forgotten Spells 
deck of the core game. 
Alice and Alhazred: these two Mages can be chosen as a Mage during 
step 8 of the Setup described in the core game rulebook.
Rooms: the Rooms in this box (and the corresponding Activation 
Token) can replace Rooms in the core game that have the same Name 
without regard to the italicized part, to have a Lodge with more 
varied Effects; for example, you can replace the Black Rose Room of 
the core game with the Deep Black Rose Room, or the Cemetery of 
the core game with the Whales Cemetery.
No Quest Cards or Event Cards from Black Rose Wars are included 
in a Duel game. Materials that generate Effects (usually Spells and 
Rooms) that call upon these types of cards must never be included 
in the game.
B L A C K R O S E WA R S I N T O D U E L 
Material from the other products in the Black Rose Wars line can be 
used to expand this game by including it in games as shown below.
School of Magic: you may replace the Void School of Magic with 
any School of Magic from the other products, making sure to include 
in the game all the materials necessary for the operation of that School 
(such as Evocations). Due to the head-to-head challenge nature of 
this game, some of the Schools of Magic that develop complex tactics 
may not be impactful in a duel. It should also be kept in mind that if a 
School of Magic involves the use of additional rules, these will need to 
be consulted in the rulebook of the Black Rose Wars/Rebirth core game 
or in the rulebook of the expansion from which the chosen School of 
Magic comes. 
Forgotten Spells: you may add any Forgotten Spells from the other 
Black Rose Wars products to the games of Duel by adding them to the 
deck during Setup. 
Mages: you may use all Mages from the other Black Rose Wars 
products in Duel games.
Rooms: you may replace the seven Rooms with Rooms from other 
Black Rose Wars products when playing Duel.
Incompatible material: Quest Cards and Event Cards from Black 
Rose Wars are not included in a Duel game. Materials that generate 
Effects (usually Spells and Rooms) that call for these types of cards may 
never be included in the game.

From the Depths
A Legendary Scenario is a special game that allows you to play an 
event from the world of Black Rose Wars: Rebirth by applying a 
specific set of new rules and using the Legendary Scenario Cards 
dedicated to it.
In From the Depths, the Mages must stop the awakening of the 
Great Old Ones, among the few entities capable of countering the 
Black Rose.
Apply the following during the regular Game Setup:
1. Steps 1 , 2 and 3 : instead of what is indicated in these points, 
arrange the Rooms as shown in the image below. 
2. Each player draws 2 Marks of the Old Ones Cards and keeps 
one, revealing it in front of themselves and putting the other at 
the bottom of the deck.
3. Place one of the 7 Idol Tokens randomly on each room.
4. Place the Event card on the side dedicated to the Legendary 
Scenario.

MARK OF THE OLD ONES CARDS
Each Mage starts the game with a Mark of the Old Ones card (see 
Additional Setup).
A Mage may use the Effect of their Mark of the Old Ones card 
throughout the game, unless otherwise stated on its text.
If, for any reason, a Mage gains a new Mark of the Old Ones card, 
they discard the previous one and apply the Effect of the new card 
from that point on.
