Basic Concepts
Models may be Opposing or Allied with one another.
ALLIED MODELS
Models under the control of the same player are considered 
Allied Models.
OPPOSING MODELS
Models under the control of two diff erent players are considered 
Opposing Models.
Walls and some other elements drawn on the Playmat (shown 
in white in the Game Area diagram to the side) are Blocking 
Elements (BE). These elements hinder Line of Sight (see below), 
but not Model Movement. Nevertheless, a Model cannot stop 
on a blocking element.
A Model is considered to have a Line of Sight (LoS) on all 
Models on the Playmat.
The LoS is considered Clean if by drawing a straight line 
between the two aff ected Models it does not pass over any BE.
The LoS is considered Obstructed if by drawing a straight line 
between the two aff ected Models it passes over at least one BE; 
an Obstructed LoS forces a Reduction (see Distance Measuring 
on the next page) to all distances measured between the two 
aff ected Models. For example, in the image on the right, if 
Gramigna wants to target Baron Doria with a Spell with Range 
6, but there is an EB between the two Models, the range of 
the Spell becomes 3.

Dice

Cubes and Pool
The game uses 8-sided dice (d8) to perform rolls.
REROLLING THE DICE
Some rules allow dice to be rerolled. Regardless of how many Eff ects or rules allow a die to be rerolled, a single die can never be 
rerolled more than once. The reroll must be made before any modifi ers are applied.
When performing an attack roll each die used in the roll must 
equal or exceed a value called Success Threshold or ST. This 
value is 5, so a die obtains a Success if it shows a result of 5 or 
higher (5+). The Success Threshold can be changed in various 
ways during the game.
Each player has at their disposal a number of colored Cubes 
that form their Pool. These Cubes will be used in various ways 
during the game, mainly to represent Instability (the power of 
Spells) and the Damage infl icted on Opposing Models. Unless 
otherwise specifi ed in a game rule and Eff ect, when a player's 
Cube is to be removed from a spot in the game (a Pool, a 
Playmat spot, etc...) it returns to the owner's Pool.

Game Flow
A game of Rebirth Skirmish is divided into Rounds. Each Round 
is divided into 4 Phases:
• Study Phase
• Action Phase
• Black Rose Phase
• Cleanup Phase
Each Adventure indicates the maximum number of Rounds for 
its duration. When a Round ends a new one begins unless the 
maximum number of Rounds indicated in the Adventure have 
been played. Once the last Round is played the game ends and 
Power Point calculations are made to determine the winner.
CROWN TOKEN
This token indicates who is the fi rst player for the Round.
Study Phase
In this phase, players decide how much Instability (the power 
with which they fuel their powers) to invest in their Spells. 
Each player takes up to 3 cubes from their Pool; these cubes 
represent Instability. The player places the Instability as they 
prefer on their Mage Sheet by assigning it to the Spells indicated 
on it; any number of an Instability can be assigned to the same 
Spell (even none).

A Spell that has Instability assigned to it can be cast during the

When all players have decided how to assign Instability, the 
Study Phase ends and you move on to the Action Phase.
Action Phase
Starting with the player with the Crown and proceeding 
clockwise, each player performs a Turn.
In a Turn, a player must activate their Mage and may choose to 
activate one of their Followers who does not have a Persistence 
Token on their Card; they may activate these two Models in any 
order they choose. When their Turn begins, the player takes the 
topmost Persistence Token in the stack on their Mage Sheet and 
places it on the Card of the Follower they chose to Activate; if 
they do not activate any Follower they simply set them aside.
When the player has resolved the activations of their Models, 
the Turn passes to the player to their left; this continues until 
each player has removed all Persistence Tokens from their Mage 
sheet.
ACTIONS
Actions allow Models to infl uence the game in diff erent ways. 
When a Model is Activated, it can perform a number of Actions 
equal to the Actions feature indicated on its Sheet. The player 
who controls it chooses which Actions the Model performs, 
and may choose the same Action several times during a single 
Activation.
The actions are:
MOVEMENT
The Model can move a maximum number of cm equal to its 
Speed feature.
During this Movement the Model can be moved freely without 
regard to the BEs (shown in white in the Playmat diagram 
attached to this document).
The Model can end its Movement with its base partially on a 
BE, as long as the center of its base is not on a BE; the Model is 
considered Hindered as long as it is in that position (see Physical 
Attack).
If the Model's Movement ends with the center of its base above 
a BE, you must follow these rules:
• The Model must be moved the minimum distance possible 
that brings the center of its base not above the BE on which 
it sits.
• After the move, the Model should have its base partially 
on the BE.

If during its Movement Action a Model does not stand with 
the center of its base on a BE and is in an Opposing Model's 
Engagement Area, it becomes Engaged and the Movement
